Toepen

Toepen is a Dutch card game you'll see in every brown café in the Netherlands. You play with 4 cards and only the last trick counts. It's all about bluffing. Knock on the table and the stakes go up. Do the others dare to stay in?

The rules are simple. The game only gets exciting once you start knocking. Below you'll learn step by step how it works. At the bottom you'll find how to turn it into a drinking game.

Setup

Toepen is played with 32 cards. Take a normal deck and remove all 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s and 6s and the jokers. From every suit you keep the 7, 8, 9, 10, jack, queen, king and ace.

  1. Write everyone's name on a piece of paper. That's where you keep track of penalty points.
  2. Agree on how many penalty points knock you out. Usually that's 10. Want a longer game? Play to 15.
  3. Pick a dealer. The dealer shuffles and gives everyone 4 cards.
  4. The rest of the deck stays face down on the table.

Toepen works best with 4 to 6 players. You can play with 2 or 3, but there's less bluffing.

The order of the cards

In Toepen the order is different from what you're used to. The 10 is the highest card and the jack the lowest.

  1. 10 (highest)
  2. 9
  3. 8
  4. 7
  5. Ace
  6. King
  7. Queen
  8. Jack (lowest)

There are no trumps. A card from another suit never wins, even if it's higher.

How to play

Every round has 4 tricks. Everyone plays one card per trick.

  1. The player to the left of the dealer leads. They put one card face up on the table.
  2. The others take turns to play a card. You have to follow suit. A heart is led and you have hearts? Then you have to play a heart.
  3. Don't have that suit? Then play any card you like. That card can't win the trick.
  4. The highest card of the suit that was led wins the trick. Whoever wins the trick leads the next one.
  5. You play 4 tricks like this. Only the last trick counts. Whoever wins it, wins the round.
  6. All other players get penalty points. Without a knock that's 1 point.
  7. The dealer moves one seat to the left and a new round starts.

So the first 3 tricks don't count. But they do matter. Whoever wins a trick leads the next one. That's how you decide which suit the last trick starts with. Save your strongest card for the end.

Knocking: raising the stakes

This is where the game gets its name. "Toep" is Dutch for knock. During a round, any player can knock on the table and say "toep". The stake then goes up by 1 point.

  • After a knock, every other player chooses: stay in or fold.
  • Stay in? Then you keep playing for the higher stake.
  • Fold? Then you put your cards away. You get the penalty points of the stake before the knock. You sit out the rest of the round.
  • After you knock, someone else has to knock first. Only then can you knock again.
  • Everyone folds? Then you win the round straight away.

An example

The stake is 1. Lisa knocks, so the stake goes to 2. Tom folds and gets 1 penalty point. Sem stays in. One trick later Sem knocks. The stake goes to 3. Lisa stays in. Sem wins the last trick? Then Lisa gets 3 penalty points.

Bluffing

You don't need good cards to knock. A knock with bad cards can make the others fold. But if they stay in, you're stuck with a high stake and nothing in your hand. That's exactly what makes Toepen so much fun.

Dirty laundry

Only face cards in your hand? Then you've got dirty laundry ("vuile was" in Dutch). Face cards are the jack, queen, king and ace. Many groups also count 3 face cards and a 7 as dirty laundry.

  1. Call dirty laundry before the first card is played.
  2. Put your cards away face down and take 4 new ones from the deck.
  3. Someone doesn't believe you? They can check the cards you put away.
  4. Your dirty laundry was real? Then the doubter gets 1 penalty point.
  5. It wasn't? Then you get 1 penalty point and keep playing with your old cards.

Who wins?

Reached the agreed number of penalty points? Then you're out. The others keep playing. The last player standing wins the game.

Prefer a shorter game? Stop as soon as the first player is out. They've lost.

House rules

Every Dutch pub and every student house plays Toepen a little differently. These are the differences you'll come across most.

  • When you can knock: in some groups at any moment, in others only when it's your turn.
  • The limit: 10 penalty points is the most common. With 15 a game lasts longer.
  • Dirty laundry: only with 4 face cards, or also with 3 face cards and a 7.

Toepen as a drinking game

Toepen is a game you play over a drink anyway. Want to turn it into a proper drinking game? Pick one or more of these options. Agree on them before you start.

Round losers drink

Every penalty point is also a sip. Lose a round with a stake of 3? Then you get 3 penalty points and take 3 sips. Fold and you drink the points you get.

The loser of the game drinks

Whoever hits the penalty point limit first downs an adtje, their whole drink in one go. Or buys the next round.

The winner hands out

Whoever wins the last trick hands out the stake in sips. They can split them over several players. That way the winner is the boss.

With an anytimer at stake

The winner of the game gets an anytimer from the loser. They can use it any time they like.
